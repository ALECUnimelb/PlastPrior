## Fig. 2

Fig. 2 | PBMT score coverage and concordance with PlastChem. (a) Availability of accepted computational and mapped PlastChem scores among 15,125 compounds. Bars partition the full inventory into four mutually exclusive categories: computational only, shared, PlastChem only and neither. (b–e) Paired score comparisons for toxicity, bioaccumulation, mobility and persistence, with shared n = 2,963, 270, 135 and 234, respectively. Bars above the matrices show exact agreement, nonzero |Δ| ≤0.5, 0.5<|Δ|≤1 and |Δ|>1, from left to right. Numerical labels are shown only for counts of at least 2 and at least 2.5% of the largest cell count within each matrix; unlabelled cells may therefore contain nonzero counts. Outlines mark exact score agreement. Shading represents within-domain fractions, scaled separately for each domain. Mean |Δ| is the mean absolute ordinal-score difference. Bioaccumulation uses selected QSAR BCF evidence and excludes the structural halogen rule. Missing scores are excluded from paired comparisons; supported zeros remain valid. These descriptive comparisons do not establish predictive accuracy.

## Fig. 4

Fig. 4 | Sensitivity of rankings and priority tiers. (a) One weighted input is removed at a time from its domain and total, without rescaling. High retention is the fraction of 265 baseline High compounds still satisfying H>10 and E>=5; rank correlation is Spearman ρ for total scores. (b) Baseline and six alternative scenarios compare rank correlation, overall tier agreement and High-set Jaccard similarity. Equal PBMT weights are all 2; other scenarios alter inventory scoring, cap exposure at 8, or shift the strict High hazard threshold to H>9 or H>11. Exposure remains >=5; the moderate lower hazard boundary remains 6. The moderate upper boundary follows the selected High hazard cutoff. Scores are not changed in the two threshold-only scenarios. These are deterministic stress tests, not uncertainty intervals.

## Fig. 5

Fig. 5 | Policy concordance and stable candidates. (a) Prevalence of strong-action status by priority category, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals; the high-versus-low odds ratio is 8.42 (Wald 95% confidence interval, 6.49–10.94). (b) Capture of strong-action compounds at a common 1.75% screening fraction (265/15,125), interpolating between tied-score groups; the dashed line marks random expectation. (c) Regulatory context of 62 stable high-priority candidates outside strong-action categories. The 125 stable High compounds satisfy H>10 and E>=5 at baseline and under all four scoring perturbations: equal PBMT weights, linear inventory scoring, halved inventory weight and exposure capped at 8. No additional total-score threshold is applied. (d) NORMAN SusDat annotation among the one candidate without a matched GIC listing: it matches SusDat without a resolved platform prediction. Absolute counts are shown at each step. The final platform count is conditional on that single match. Concordance is not independent validation, and absent annotation does not establish analytical impossibility.

## Extended Data Fig. 3

Extended Data Fig. 3 | Five-level priority landscape and component evidence profiles among 15,125 compounds. (a) Distribution of the integer priority score, defined as the sum of separately rounded weighted H and E scores; labels show score-bin totals. Solid colors indicate priority level, and the inset shows levels and their regulatory context. (b) Hazard–exposure matrix with exact counts in all 289 integer-score cells. Dashed lines and equal-thickness guide bars mark L = 0–3, M = 4–7, H = 8–11 and vH = 12–16. Level 1 comprises vH/vH pairs (101 compounds); Level 2 comprises vH/H pairs in either order (1,119); Level 3 comprises the remaining pairs with vH and the H/H pair (3,482); Level 4 comprises other pairs with at least one H (4,702); Level 5 comprises the remainder (5,721). Color encodes level only, without count-dependent shading. The integer cells have equal widths; because scores include both 0 and 16, the vH band contains five integer values while the other bands contain four. Classification changes only figure labels, not stored scores or ranks. (c) Unweighted PBMT component profiles distinguish missing evidence from supported zero and retain curated fractional scores. (d) Five unweighted exposure-dimension profiles; food contact and biomonitoring form one joint dimension. Partial evidence denotes a joint dimension with only one available indicator, whose contribution remains in E. A numerical zero does not establish absence of hazard or exposure.
