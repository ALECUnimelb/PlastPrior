## Fig. 1 | PlastPrior workflow and analysis populations

**a,** Source records are harmonized and resolved to chemical identities, yielding 15,125 unique compounds. Structural quality control identifies 11,236 QSAR-eligible compounds; the other 3,889 remain in the inventory and can retain other usable evidence. **b,** Hazard integrates persistence (P), bioaccumulation (B), mobility (M) and toxicity (T), with weights 2, 2, 1 and 3, respectively. Independent evidence includes model predictions and applicable structural rules; curated evidence is used according to the recorded selection policy. Six exposure inputs—inventory presence, production/market, patents, literature, food contact and human biomonitoring—form five dimensions, with the last two combined. Unavailable evidence remains missing, distinct from an evidence-supported zero. **c,** The export retains all compounds and their provenance. Formal prioritization and ranking require complete hazard and exposure evidence: 4,244 compounds qualify, while 10,881 are labelled insufficient evidence. The dashboard presents both scores and supporting records. QSAR eligibility and ranking eligibility are different criteria.

## Fig. 2 | Coverage and concordance of independent and PlastChem hazard evidence

**a,** Mutually exclusive evidence-coverage categories for all 15,125 compounds in each PBMT dimension. Independent evidence denotes usable candidates after identity isolation, including structural rules for bioaccumulation, rather than QSAR predictions alone. A recorded score of zero counts as available evidence; a missing candidate does not. **b–e,** Paired comparisons for toxicity (n = 2,963), bioaccumulation (n = 280), mobility (n = 135) and persistence (n = 234), respectively. Matrix shading encodes compound counts, and outlined cells indicate identical scores. Numerals are suppressed below the plotting threshold for legibility; an unlabelled cell can therefore contain a small nonzero count. All counts are supplied in the source table. The rows and columns retain the independent and PlastChem score scales, respectively. Bars summarize absolute score differences: exact agreement (0), small differences (>0 to 0.5), moderate differences (>0.5 to 1) and large differences (>1), in dark green, light green, ochre and red. The mean absolute differences are 0.75, 0.66, 0.76 and 0.55, respectively. Percentages may not sum to 100% after rounding. Concordance describes these paired evidence sources and does not establish predictive accuracy against an independent experimental benchmark.

## Fig. 3 | Priority distribution and evidence profiles

**a,** Distribution of total priority scores in one-point intervals among the 4,244 compounds eligible for ranking. The inset instead covers the full inventory (n = 15,125): its inner ring shows priority status (365 low, 1,828 moderate, 2,051 high and 10,881 insufficient evidence), and its outer ring subdivides each status by GIC-derived regulatory context. **b,** Joint hazard and exposure scores among eligible compounds; point area represents the number of compounds at each coordinate. Tier boundaries require both total-score and component thresholds: moderate priority requires total ≥6 and hazard and exposure each ≥2; high priority requires total ≥12 and both components ≥4. **c,** Mean hazard and exposure contributions within each total-score interval, using the same eligible cohort. **d,e,** Hazard and exposure profiles across all 15,125 compounds. Each row has the full-inventory denominator and distinguishes unavailable evidence from an observed zero. The combined food-contact/biomonitoring dimension is unavailable when either required component is missing. Hazard score 0.25 is the low-confidence category specified by the scoring system. Numbers label compound counts; very small segments may be unlabelled for legibility. Insufficient-evidence compounds are retained in the inset and component profiles, but are not assigned a total score or placed in the ranked distributions.

## Fig. 4 | Sensitivity of priorities to observed inputs and scoring choices

All comparisons use the fixed baseline cohort of 4,244 eligible compounds; the 10,881 insufficient-evidence compounds are excluded without imputation. **a,** Each of ten observed, weighted inputs is removed in turn, without rescaling the remaining inputs. Red points show the fraction of the 2,051 baseline high-priority compounds that remain high priority; teal points show Spearman rank correlation with the baseline total score. This deliberate removal of an observed contribution is a sensitivity perturbation, not replacement of missing evidence with zero. **b,** Spearman correlation, tier agreement and high-priority Jaccard overlap under alternative scenarios. Scenarios assign weight 2 to each PBMT dimension; assign 0.5 inventory points per listing capped at 2; halve the inventory contribution; cap total exposure at 8; or change the high-priority total threshold to 11 or 13. Unless explicitly varied, total-score thresholds remain 6 and 12, and the moderate/high component gates remain 2 and 4. Jaccard overlap is the intersection divided by the union of the baseline and scenario high-priority sets. Correlations are displayed as percentages for consistency with the other metrics; these metrics describe different aspects of stability.

## Fig. 5 | GIC context, matched-size screening and candidates for review

The analysis uses the 4,244 compounds with complete baseline evidence. The operational strong-action group comprises GIC statuses prohibited/phase-out, restricted/authorisation-required and candidate/high-concern (551 compounds); it is a database-derived screening grouping, not a determination of current legal status. **a,** Strong-action proportions by priority tier, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals: high, 367/2,051 (17.9%); moderate, 127/1,828 (6.9%); low, 57/365 (15.6%). These proportions are not monotonic. The high-versus-low odds ratio is 1.18 (log-Wald 95% CI 0.87–1.60), and should not be described as a clear enrichment over the low tier. **b,** Fraction of the 551 strong-action compounds captured at a matched screening fraction of 2,051/4,244 (48.3%), using the integrated, exposure-only, inventory-removed integrated and hazard-only scores. Capture is 70.5%, 69.7%, 69.1% and 65.2%, respectively. Linear interpolation within tied-score groups estimates capture at exactly the same screening size; this is not the official inclusive top-N selection rule. The dashed line indicates the random-selection expectation. **c,** Regulatory context of 1,193 stable high-priority compounds outside the operational strong-action group. Stability requires baseline total ≥13 and total ≥12 under all four score perturbations in Fig. 4, with hazard and exposure each ≥4 throughout. Of these candidates, 1,028 have monitoring/reporting or hazard-classification context, 149 have inventory/registration/use-only context, and 16 have no linked GIC listing. Percentages are rounded independently. **d,** Among those 16 compounds, 15 have no NORMAN SusDat match and one is matched but has unresolved platform annotation. Absence of a link does not establish absence from regulation or analytical detectability. These are descriptive comparisons within a selected evidence-complete cohort, not an independent external validation.

## Extended Data Fig. 1 | Record resolution and source overlap

**a,** Record-level flow through identifier reporting, recovery and resolution, followed by compound deduplication and structural QSAR eligibility. Of 87,209 harmonized records, 57,839 contribute to the retained compound inventory and 29,370 do not; deduplication collapses 42,714 records to yield 15,125 compounds. Of these compounds, 11,236 satisfy the structural QSAR-eligibility rules and 3,889 do not. Units change from records to compounds at deduplication; widths should be interpreted with this transition in mind. **b,** Source overlap reconstructed from retained record-to-compound links using full standardized InChIKeys. Side bars show source-specific unique compound counts. Filled dots and connecting lines identify exact source combinations, and upper bars give their compound counts. FCChumon S1 and S2 are separate source tables. The 23 exact intersections with at least 100 compounds contain 13,221 compounds (87.4% of the inventory); 183 smaller intersections containing 1,904 compounds are omitted. All intersections are retained in the accompanying source table. Source-membership absence denotes absence of a retained record link; it is not a zero hazard or exposure score.

## Extended Data Fig. 2 | Hazard-evidence availability and positive exposure patterns

**a,** Exact overlap of usable final PBMT evidence across all 15,125 compounds. Membership means a non-missing canonical score, including an evidence-supported zero. All four dimensions are available for 8,550 compounds; 2,087 have none. Numbers, rather than ellipse areas, encode intersection sizes. **b,** Exact patterns of positive exposure scores among the 7,951 compounds with complete exposure evidence. The other 7,174 compounds are excluded from this panel because their missing inputs cannot be classified as negative. Five dimensions are shown: inventory presence, production/market, patents, literature and combined food contact/biomonitoring. A positive dimension requires score >0; the combined dimension is positive if either of its two observed components is positive. The 22 nonempty positive patterns contain 7,946 compounds; five complete-evidence compounds have zero across all five dimensions and are omitted from the positive-pattern bars. The red upper bar marks the all-five-positive intersection. Side bars count positive compounds per dimension. Dots and vertical connectors encode the same exact combinations as the upper bars.

## Extended Data Fig. 3 | Positive-input breadth and prevalence within priority tiers

Both panels use the 4,244 compounds with complete evidence: low n = 365, moderate n = 1,828 and high n = 2,051. **a,** Within-tier distribution of the number of positive-scoring inputs across four hazard and six exposure inputs. Median counts are 4, 6 and 7 in the low, moderate and high tiers, respectively. **b,** Within-tier proportions with a positive contribution from each input. A positive contribution is an observed score greater than zero; missing values are not counted as observed zeros. The 10,881 insufficient-evidence compounds are excluded from both panels. Percentages are rounded, and small heatmap cells may be unlabelled. Because priority tiers are derived from these same inputs, the association is descriptive and partly built into the scoring procedure.

## Extended Data Fig. 4 | Dashboard view of a current PlastPrior result

Unaltered browser capture of the dashboard after loading the paired publication CSV and detail SQLite database from the locked analysis. The example is decabromodiphenyl oxide (InChIKey WHHGLZMJPXIBIX-UHFFFAOYSA-N), with hazard 16, exposure 10 and total priority 26. The displayed literature count is 655 from NCBI PubMed Title/Abstract. The view links chemical identity, source membership, hazard evidence, exposure inputs, GIC screening context and model-predicted analytical platform. Selected score summaries and individual model outputs are distinct: for example, structural rules can contribute to the final bioaccumulation score even when the displayed BCF prediction is low. The NORMAN platform annotation denotes predicted amenability, not experimental detection. The results list groups some chemical families, so its displayed item count is not the total number of inventory compounds. The upload header is excluded from the captured element; no pixel-level edits were made.
